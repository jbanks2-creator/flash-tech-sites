## The headline: your tasks never leave your browser

**We do not have your data. Not "we don't sell it" — we do not receive it, and we have no way to look at it.**

TaskApp is a single HTML file that runs entirely in your browser. Everything you type — tasks, domains, projects, notes, files — is stored in your own browser's local storage on your own device. There is no account, no login, no cloud, no sync, no server that knows you exist.

- **No analytics.** No Google Analytics, no Plausible, no pixel, no heatmap.
- **No telemetry.** The app does not report crashes, usage, counts or versions.
- **No third-party scripts.** Nothing is loaded from anyone else's server.
- **No cookies for tracking.** The app uses browser storage to keep your own data and your theme choice — that is it.
- **No network calls at all.** Not on first run, not on save, not on exit. (Links you click inside the app open in your browser — the app itself never calls out.)

**Check it yourself.** Right-click → View Source, or open the file in a text editor. It is one file, unminified enough to read. There is no `fetch`, no `XMLHttpRequest`, no `<script src="https://…">`. You can also run it with the network disconnected — it works exactly the same.

That is the point: a task list is a private thing. A tool that cannot leak your client list, your pricing, or what you are working on is worth more than one that promises not to.

**If a future version ever adds a network feature** — licence activation, update checks, sync — this statement will be rewritten and dated before that version ships, and the change will be announced. It will not be added quietly.
